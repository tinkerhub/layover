How it ran

Mentoring by night, judging by morning.

The same 11 mentors, working professionals from industry, ran both tracks. Through the night they checked in on every team; in the morning they became the judges. A small app kept it fair: mentors rotated so every team got fresh eyes each round, and judges sat out any team they knew personally.

TRACK 1

Mentoring

10:30 PM – 6:00 AM
  1. Check-in 1 · Idea & direction10:30 PM. What are you building, what's the travel connection, what will work by morning?
  2. Check-in 2 · Progress & demo2:30 AM. Show what runs. Blocked? On track? Early call for the final pool.
  3. Check-in 3 · Final review6:00 AM. Demo-ready? Strongest and weakest part, and a recommendation for the finalists.
  4. Help on demandAny mentor could flag a team that needed hardware, software, AI or design help; the right mentors were alerted on their phones.
300+check-ins
Everyteam visited
~6 minper check-in

Mentor notes never counted toward marks. They helped teams overnight and gave the judges a must-see list.

TRACK 2

Judging

7:00 AM – 9:30 AM
  1. Round 1 · Demo booths7:00 AM. Science-fair style: judges walked the floor, saw every project and nominated the ones the panel had to see.
  2. Shortlist8:00 AM. Booth nominations and the night's notes became 13 finalists.
  3. Round 2 · Finalist pitches8:15 AM. Each finalist pitched and demoed to the full panel.
  4. Scoring & deliberationEvery judge scored every finalist out of 50: what was built 20, creativity 10, travel connection 10, execution 5, demo 5.
13finalists
11judges
143score sheets

Averages were also checked against judge-normalised scores, so a generous or strict judge couldn't tilt the result. The panel made the final call.

Overview

One theme. No rulebook.

LAYOVER is an overnight student hackathon at TinkerSpace Calicut, bringing together up to 200 students to build something, anything, around the theme of travel. Software, hardware, AI, games, experiments, or something completely unnecessary but fun.

There's no requirement to solve a real-world problem or become a business. The only expectation: something gets built and demonstrated by the end of the night. Teams check in from 5:00 PM, build from 6:00 PM straight through to 6:00 AM, and present to judges as the sun comes up.

“

A weird travel-themed gadget can score just as high as a practical travel app, as long as travel is the actual point of the build, not a word tacked on afterward.

FAQ

Everything you're wondering.

Eligibility, teams, building, and the night itself, answered.

Who can participate?
LAYOVER is open to community members aged 15–25. Working professionals are not eligible for this community participation.
Can I participate alone?
Yes. You can apply individually or as a team of up to 2 members.
How will participants be shortlisted?
This is a limited community opportunity. Active participation in the TinkerHub community and your profile/activity on the TinkerHub app will be considered during shortlisting.
What criteria are used for shortlisting?

Each applicant gets a community score out of 100, based on their activity in the TinkerHub app:

AreaMaxHow points are given
TinkerSpace252 per day checked in at TinkerSpace Calicut, plus 2 per Calicut event attended (capped at 20). Plus 1 per day at TinkerSpace Kochi (max 5).
TinkerHub community303 per TinkerHub event you checked in to (max 20), 3 each time you organized or mentored (max 7), and 3 if you're a campus lead.
Projects254 per project on your profile, plus 4 more if it has code, is completed, or is verified (max 25).
Profile20Points for bio, photo, GitHub (counts double), LinkedIn, skills, course, education and experience filled in, scaled to 20.
Do I need to already have a team?
No. You can apply individually and find a teammate before the event.
What do I need to build?
Anything around travel. It could be an app, AI project, hardware prototype, game, IoT project, interactive experience, or something completely experimental.
Is there a fixed problem statement?
No. There is only one broad theme: travel. You decide what to build.
Are there any technology restrictions?
No. Use software, hardware, AI, electronics, or a combination of them.
Does it have to solve a real travel problem?
No. It can be useful, weird, playful, experimental, or completely unnecessary. The only requirement is that travel is genuinely connected to your idea.
Do I need to be good at coding?
No. You can participate with whatever skills you have. The focus is on building and experimenting.
How long do we get to build?
The hackathon runs from 6:00 PM to 6:00 AM. Check-in starts at 5:00 PM.
What happens after the build?
Projects will be demonstrated and judged from 7:00 AM to 9:30 AM in two rounds.
How will projects be judged?
Projects are scored out of 50 marks across what was built, creativity, travel connection, execution, and demo experience.
What is the prize?
The overall winning team gets a fully covered Dubai trip for up to 2 people.
Is participation free?
Yes. There is no participation fee.
Can I use AI tools?
Yes. AI tools are completely allowed.
Can I bring my own hardware?
Yes. Bring your own laptop, components, hardware, and other tools you need for your project.
Can I work on an existing project?
The expectation is that you build and make meaningful progress during the hackathon. Your final project should be demonstrable by the end of the event.
What should I bring?
Bring your laptop, charger, hardware/components you need, and anything else required for your build. And be ready for an overnight build.
Will food be provided?
Yes. Food and refreshments will be arranged during the overnight event.
When and where is LAYOVER?
10–11 October 2026 at TinkerSpace Calicut, Kozhikode.
Check-in: 5:00 PM · Build: 6:00 PM–6:00 AM · Judging: 7:00 AM–9:30 AM
